# Home is an instrument that is dismissed

Home frames the First Spore inside an **Instrument**: a ruled grid, a crosshair, and
telemetry reading the real world behind it. Nothing else in the piece looks like this.
Every phase is soft and organic — bioluminescent colour, glow, soil, grain — and the
Instrument is the opposite: exact, monospaced, measured. That contrast is the point.
The viewer starts outside the specimen, at the safe distance an apparatus gives you,
and Begin is the moment that distance is taken away: the grid fades, the telemetry
retracts, the camera pushes in, and what is left is the organism with nothing between
you and it. The Instrument never returns, in any phase or on any revisit.

We chose this over three alternatives. Keeping the organic register on Home too would
have made Begin a scene change rather than a loss of distance. Carrying the Instrument
through the whole piece would have made the Narrator an instrument as well, and the
Narrator speaks to the viewer as the spore — "you begin to grow" — which is the
opposite stance. Putting a decorative mycelium behind the title, which the Figma board
originally asked for, would have meant a second world that is not the one you enter,
contradicting [ADR 0001](0001-one-continuous-world.md) and showing the viewer the
ending of Giving before they had started.

What Home shows is therefore the real First Spore in the real world, dormant and
breathing, at a slightly wider zoom than Drifting uses. Begin eases in to Drifting's
own framing rather than cutting, so the world is continuous across the threshold and
ADR 0001 holds unchanged. The cost is that Home cannot be a still image: it runs the
world, and the telemetry reads live camera and world values, which is also what makes
the Instrument honest about what it is measuring.

The title is set in a hand-drawn face whose letterforms are built from scattered dots.
Left against a ruled, mechanical frame this reads as two unrelated designs, so the
Instrument's own marks — crosshair, ticks, button frame — are drawn as stipple in the
same particulate vocabulary. The grid stays geometrically exact; only the marks the
Instrument lays on the specimen are made of the same material as the specimen.
